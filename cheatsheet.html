What to pay for anything you plan to resell in Canada — verified October 2026. Printable.

The only formula you need

MAX PAY = (sale price − platform fee − shipping) × (1 − your margin)Rule of thumb for Canadian platforms: pay no more than ⅓ of the expected sale price.

Why a third? Because the real all-in cost of selling a $60 item online in Canada is typically $18–25 once you pay the fee, the tax on the fee, and shipping — and because not every item sells at your hoped price. A ⅓ buy-in means you still profit even if you must discount 30% to move it.

The five mistakes that eat beginners alive

  • Buying at 50% "because it's a good brand." Fees + shipping + the discount you'll eventually take mean 50% is break-even, not profit.
  • Forgetting the tax on the fee. In Ontario a $300 Poshmark sale costs $60 in commission plus $7.80 HST on that commission.
  • Listing locally out of laziness on $100+ items. Local is $0 fee, but you're pricing to one buyer, not an auction market. Big-ticket electronics often net MORE on eBay after 13.6% than locally.
  • Paying for the case, not the tool. A $300 drill in a cracked case is a $120 drill. Price the worst-case condition.
  • Sinking a week into a $6 profit flip. Your hours are a cost. If net profit ÷ hours worked < minimum wage, it's a hobby, not a business.

    • Frequently asked

    What's a good profit margin for flipping in Canada?

    Target 3× your buy-in (200%+ margin) for small items under $50, and 2× for higher-ticket goods — because per-item selling effort is roughly constant, small items need higher multiples to be worth it.

    Is the ⅓ rule ever wrong?

    Yes: for $0-fee local-pickup items (Kijiji, Facebook pickup) you can safely go to 40–45% of expected resale, since there's no fee and no shipping. And for anything seasonal (Christmas lights in July), pay even less — your capital sits for months.

    How do I know what it will resell for?

    Search the SOLD listings (not asking prices) on the platform you'll use — eBay has a sold-listings filter. Asking prices are what sellers wish; sold prices are what buyers pay. The full calculator on the main page does the rest of the math for you.

    Does this work for thrift stores, or just yard sales?

    Same formula, higher buy-in. Thrift stores already price at 20–40% of retail, so your edge is condition-spotting and category knowledge (test everything), not negotiation. Estate sales: price is negotiable on day 2+.
